# Communication policy

Read this before writing documentation, decision records, or long reports. Write so the intended reader can understand the point and act on it, in their language and at the level of detail they asked for.

## Draft

- Put the main point or requested action first. Use familiar, concrete words and direct sentences.
- Prefer specific actions and evidence over hype or unsupported claims such as "world-class" or "seamless". Cut filler, clichés, buzzwords, and unclear metaphors, or replace them with the idea they stand for.
- No word is banned outright. Choose words for meaning, audience, and tone.
- Explain a necessary technical term briefly on first use when the reader may not know it. Do not simplify away distinctions that matter.
- Keep structure proportional. Use headings, lists, and summaries to help navigation, not to force a template; let paragraph and list length vary naturally.
- Keep a human voice. Humor, emphasis, or a metaphor can stay when it serves the purpose and the reader will understand it.

## Translate, simplify, summarize

Keep the source's meaning, qualifications, tone, and uncertainty. Do not add claims, drop material caveats, or turn a recommendation into a fact. Flag a phrase that has no clear equivalent. Match the requested reading level without being patronizing.

## Check before delivering

Is the main point easy to find? Is the detail right for this reader? Can vague or inflated wording be more concrete? Does the structure help? Are meaning, evidence, uncertainty, and tone intact? Use judgment, not a checklist: concision helps only while it keeps what the reader needs.
